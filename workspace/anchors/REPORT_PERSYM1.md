# CORRECTION, 2026-10-01, same day

**A fill-bar look-ahead in the limit-entry path inflated every expectancy figure in §3.2 and
Track E. It is fixed and those numbers are restated below; nothing else in this report used
that path.**

A resting limit fills mid-bar, and a 60m OHLC bar does not say whether the high came before or
after the low. The engine was crediting the 1.5R target on the fill bar itself, which silently
assumed the favourable ordering. On a test arm, **153 of 304 trades (50.3%) were booking their
target on the very bar the limit filled.** The fill bar now allows only the adverse outcome
(`lib/core.py`, `_walk`). Restated:

| arm | as first published | corrected |
|---|---|---|
| MGC prior-week golden, limit, OOS | +0.6060R, t +2.88 | **+0.2379R, t +1.027**, n=24 |
| MGC fib funded trades | 16 of 26 | **14 of 24** |
| MGC median stop under a limit | $104 | **$106** |

**The funding conclusion is unchanged** — median stop $204 under a market order vs **$106**
under a resting limit, fundable trades **6 of 33 → 14 of 24** — because those are geometry, not
path. **What changed is the direction of the trade-off:** the limit makes the rule affordable
and *weaker per unit of risk* (OOS t +1.027 vs +2.705 for the market order), where I had
reported it as affordable and stronger. Everything in §0, §1, §2, §3.1, §3.3 and §4 used
market entries and is untouched.

## 0. Three findings that cut across all four symbols

### 0.1 Taking profit at +0.8R is reliably worse — and this is the best-powered result in the project

Paired on identical trades, eight arms, ~1,000 trades each:

| symbol | entries | paired x08 − base | t | win rate base → x08 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MES | mom | −0.1074R | **−6.78** | 34.9% → 41.0% |
| MES | rev | −0.1318R | **−8.60** | 38.4% → 43.7% |
| MNQ | mom | −0.0999R | **−6.05** | 37.8% → 44.5% |
| MNQ | rev | −0.1112R | **−6.99** | 38.3% → 44.3% |
| MGC | mom | −0.0745R | **−4.30** | 38.2% → 44.6% |
| MGC | rev | −0.1213R | **−7.40** | 40.6% → 45.6% |
| MCL | mom | −0.0680R | **−3.45** | 37.9% → 45.9% |
| MCL | rev | −0.0609R | **−3.12** | 39.9% → 48.1% |

Eight for eight, every t past −3. Exiting at 0.8R **raises the win rate by 5 to 8 percentage
points and costs 0.06 to 0.13R per trade.** This is the project's own rule — *win rate and payoff
always go together* — demonstrated as cleanly as it can be: you buy more winners and pay for them
by capping the payoff at 0.8 instead of 1.5, and the extra winners do not cover it.

If you only remember one thing from this round: **a strategy that "wins more often" after you move
the target in is usually a worse strategy.** Ask for the payoff before you believe the win rate.

### 0.2 Stop-to-breakeven at +0.8R does nothing, because it almost never fires

OQ2 was the hub's single most promising untested idea. Paired, it is flat:

| symbol | entries | paired be08 − base | t | % of trades the rule even changed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MGC | mom | +0.0162R | +1.74 | 6.0% |
| MCL | mom | +0.0152R | +1.28 | 7.1% |
| MCL | rev | +0.0107R | +0.93 | 6.6% |
| MNQ | mom | +0.0061R | +0.65 | 5.4% |
| MES | mom | −0.0008R | −0.09 | 4.9% |
| MNQ | rev | −0.0082R | −0.84 | 5.1% |
| MES | rev | −0.0085R | −0.91 | 4.3% |
| MGC | rev | −0.0123R | −1.24 | 4.1% |

Four positive, four negative, best t +1.74 against a 2.327 bar. The reason is the last column:
with a 1.5R target and a 12-bar hold, only **4–7% of trades ever reach +0.8R, fail to reach 1.5R,
and then come back to the entry.** The evidence that motivated OQ2 came from six live fills — a
sample small enough that 5% of trades can look like all of them.

What breakeven *does* do is cut drawdown, and on MES that is the difference between a live account
and a dead one: on the `mom` entries the baseline peaks at **$3,012 of drawdown against the $2,800
floor — the account is dead** — while the 0.8R full exit holds it to $2,563 and survives. So the
0.8R exit buys survival with expectancy. That is a real trade-off, not an edge.

### 0.3 The $120 risk cap, not the absence of an edge, is what blocks most of these strategies

| study | trades the rule generated | trades the budget could size |
|---|---|---|
| MES daily 20-day breakout | 70 | **0** |
| MNQ daily 20-day breakout | 65 | **0** |
| MES / MNQ daily, all four arms | 252 | **1** |
| MGC fib, market entry | 33 | 6 |
| MGC fib, resting limit | 26 | **16** |

A 1-ATR stop on a daily MES bar is roughly $300–400 per contract. The budget is $120. **Daily
index trend-following is arithmetically impossible at this account size** — not weakly profitable,
not unproven: unfundable, 1 trade out of 252. That is a sizing fact, and no amount of further
backtesting changes it.

## 1. MES — Micro E-mini S&P 500

**Tested:** exit management (OQ2), 20-day daily breakout and 2σ daily reversion over 7.4 years.

| arm | IS | OOS | t | win / payoff | halves | funded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| daily 20-day breakout | n=108, −0.0169R | n=70, **+0.1113R** | +0.701 | 44.3% / 1.51 | +0.325 / −0.102 | **0 of 70** |
| daily 2σ reversion | n=85, −0.0175R | n=60, +0.0187R | +0.107 | 36.7% / 1.78 | −0.038 / +0.075 | 1 of 60 |

The breakout is the right shape — positive expectancy, payoff above 1, and it beat a coin flip in
**10 of 10** placebo draws, with a shift-null z of +1.37. But it is under the bar, its two halves
disagree in sign (+0.325 then −0.102), and **not one of its 70 trades fits the risk budget.**

Exit management on MES is the clearest case of the §0.1 trap: the 0.8R exit lifts the win rate from
34.9% to 41.0% and costs −0.1074R per trade at t −6.78 on the momentum entries, −0.1318R at t −8.60
on the reversion entries. Breakeven is flat (−0.0008R).

**Verdict for MES: nothing tradeable.** The one promising idea is unaffordable, and MES is also the
only symbol whose baseline exit policy breaches the $2,800 floor outright.

## 2. MNQ — Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100

**Tested:** the same as MES.

| arm | IS | OOS | t | win / payoff | halves | funded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| daily 20-day breakout | n=110, +0.0382R | n=65, **+0.1310R** | +0.777 | 41.5% / 1.73 | **+0.108 / +0.153** | **0 of 65** |
| daily 2σ reversion | n=86, −0.0804R | n=57, −0.1159R | −0.657 | 31.6% / 1.80 | +0.154 / −0.377 | 0 of 57 |

The 20-day breakout on MNQ is **the most internally consistent positive signal in either study.**
It is the only arm anywhere in ANCH1 or PERSYM1 whose out-of-sample halves are both positive *and*
similar in size (+0.108, +0.153), its payoff is 1.73, it beat a coin flip 10/10, and it is positive
in-sample too rather than flipping sign. It still does not clear 2.327 (t +0.777 on 65 trades), and
it is **completely unfundable.**

Buying 2σ dips on MNQ loses, as pre-registered: −0.1159R out of sample with a 31.6% win rate. The
1.80 payoff is not enough to carry a win rate that low. Note the trap in reverse here — a *good*
payoff with a bad win rate also loses.

**Verdict for MNQ: the best-behaved signal found, and the least usable.** The right follow-up is
not a different rule but a smaller instrument or a tighter, structure-based daily stop — and a
longer out-of-sample slice, since 7.4 years yields only 65 breakout trades.

## 3. MGC — Micro Gold

**Tested:** exit management, the value-area edge (OQ3), and the funding fix for the ANCH1 candidate.

### 3.1 Value-area edge → prior POC (OQ3): fails, and fails informatively

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| in-sample | n=164, **−0.2259R**, t −3.713 |
| out-of-sample | n=91, **+0.0148R**, t +0.144 |
| win rate / payoff | **54.9% / 0.85** |
| OOS halves | +0.141 / −0.109 |
| direction placebo | coin flip averaged **+0.2553R**; the real rule beat it **1 time in 10** |
| shift-null z | +1.66 |

A complete sign flip between the two halves of the data — strongly losing in-sample, flat
out-of-sample — which is what noise looks like. Two details are worth keeping:

- **54.9% winners at a 0.85 payoff nets +0.0148R.** More than half the trades won and the strategy
  made essentially nothing. This is the single clearest illustration in the study of why a win rate
  quoted alone is useless.
- **A coin flip beat it 9 times out of 10.** Whatever the value-area edge contributes on MGC, it is
  worse than choosing the direction at random on the same bars.

OQ3 is retired.

### 3.2 The ANCH1 candidate: the funding wall is now solved

ANCH1 found MGC Fibonacci trend-failure at the prior week's golden pocket (OOS n=33, +0.4324R,
t +2.705) and it failed on money: a next-open market order gave a median $204 stop against a $120
budget, so the account could size only 6 of 33 trades. Replacing it with a **resting limit at the
level**:

| entry | trades | median stop | vs $120 cap | funded | refused |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| market, next open ±1 tick | 33 | 20.41 pts = **$204** | over | **6** | 27 |
| resting limit at the level | 24 | 10.63 pts = **$106** | **under** | **14** | 10 |

The limit **halves the stop, brings it inside the budget, and raises fundable trades from 6 to 14**,
at the cost of signals that expire unfilled (24 fills against 33 market trades). Its expectancy is
also *lower*: OOS +0.2379R at t +1.027 against +0.4324R at t +2.705 for the market order. So the
limit buys affordability and pays for it in edge — the opposite of what I first reported, before the
fill-bar look-ahead was closed (see the correction at the top). Those expectancy figures remain
in-sample-contaminated in any case, since the rule was chosen using these very bars.

### 3.3 Exit management

MGC is the one symbol where breakeven-at-0.8R leans positive in **both** halves (+0.0141, +0.0183,
paired +0.0162R at t +1.74) — the best of the eight arms and still under the bar. The 0.8R full exit
costs −0.0745R at t −4.30.

**Verdict for MGC: still the only symbol with a live candidate.** The engineering obstacle is gone;
the statistical one is not. It needs fresh bars, which means a 60m gold history longer than Yahoo's
730-day cap.

## 4. MCL — Micro WTI Crude Oil

**Tested:** exit management, and the parked momentum finding (OQ5).

| timeframe | IS | OOS | t | win / payoff | halves | shift z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 240m | n=197, **−0.1192R**, t −1.437 | n=135, +0.0521R | +0.496 | 41.5% / 1.54 | +0.037 / +0.067 | +1.37 |
| 60m | n=270, **−0.1327R**, t −2.145 | n=174, **−0.0879R** | −1.163 | 35.6% / 1.48 | +0.041 / −0.216 | +0.07 |

OQ5's parked claim was *97% of observations positive, beat control on both session arms, t 2.97.*
**It does not replicate.** On 60m the rule loses in-sample *and* out-of-sample. On 240m it loses
in-sample and goes mildly positive out-of-sample — a sign flip, not a confirmation. The pre-registered
prediction that 240m would beat 60m held; the prediction that both would be positive did not.

Note also that MCL is the only symbol where the 60m momentum rule is *fundable* (137 of 174 trades),
because crude's tick is $1 and its ATR in points is small — and the fundable one is the losing one.

Exit management on MCL: breakeven +0.0152R (t +1.28) on momentum entries, +0.0107R (t +0.93) on
reversion; the 0.8R exit is the least bad of the four symbols at −0.0680R / −0.0609R, but still
clearly negative. MCL also shows the largest win-rate inflation from the 0.8R exit — 37.9% to 45.9%
— and still loses by it.

OQ5 is retired.

## 6. What to do next

1. **Stop testing exit overlays on this account.** §0.1 and §0.2 close OQ2 in both directions with
   ~8,000 paired trades. The question is answered.
2. **The next real question is sizing, not signals.** Two of the three most promising ideas in this
   project die on the $120 cap, not on expectancy. Either the risk policy or the timeframe has to
   change; measuring more rules at this cap will keep producing unfundable winners.
3. **MGC fib trend-failure with a limit entry, on bars this study has not seen.** The rule and the
   fill mechanism are both settled now. All it needs is out-of-sample data, which means a 60m gold
   source beyond the 730-day Yahoo cap.
4. **MNQ daily 20-day breakout deserves a longer slice**, not a variant sweep. 65 out-of-sample
   trades at +0.1310R with both halves positive is the most honest lead here; more years would
   settle it, and a wider search would only burn the bar.
